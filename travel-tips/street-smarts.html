Travel tips

Street smarts for visitors to Egypt

Do’s, don’ts and how to deal with people on the street, from a local guide who lives in Cairo.

The guide in 11 slides

Egypt is welcoming, and most people you meet are kind and curious. A few simple habits make your first days smoother. Swipe through, then read the details below.

The details

Nine habits, in the order you will need them.

DO

Smile, then say “La, shukran”

“La, shukran” means no, thank you. Say it once, calmly, with a smile, and keep walking. You do not need to explain or argue.

DO

Ask the price first

For rides, photos with animals and any extra, agree the price and what it includes before you start. It avoids every awkward conversation afterwards.

DON’T

Accept “free” gifts

A free scarf, a bracelet or a “special shop” usually ends with a request for money. Most people are simply friendly, but it is always fine to say no.

DO

Keep small notes for tips

Tipping (baksheesh) for small services is normal in Egypt. Carry small notes so you do not have to ask for change.

DO

Dress respectfully

Cover shoulders and knees in mosques, churches and villages. Take your shoes off in mosques, and women may want a light scarf for the hair.

DO

Ask before photographing people

Ask first, especially with women and children. A smile and a gesture toward your camera is enough, and most people are happy to say yes.

DON’T

Photograph sensitive places

Military sites, police, checkpoints, bridges and government buildings are not for photos. Drones need official permission, so leave yours at home.

DON’T

Break the law

Drugs are illegal and the penalties are heavy. Alcohol is for licensed venues such as hotels and bars, never for the street.

DO

Move around smartly

Use ride-hailing apps, or agree the taxi price before you get in. Keep a copy of your passport with you and drink bottled water.

Rules, permits and prices can change. Check current rules with official sources before you travel, and when in doubt, ask me.
